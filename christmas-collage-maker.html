Christmas photo collage ideas

Six ideas that work with any camera roll. Pick the layout first, then the photos.

📈

Family through the years

One photo from each Christmas, oldest to newest, in a 4-photo or 9-photo grid. Same wall, same people, a different year — add “Merry Christmas” in the corner and keep the spacing wide so faces stay visible.

🍪

Baking day

Gingerbread, cookies, flour on the counter: six photos in a 2 × 3 grid with a retro or glow filter. A short line like “Baking memories” works better than a long caption.

🎄

Tree decorating

Before, during and after in a 2 × 2 grid. If the room is dark, lift the exposure with a filter and keep the tree in the same corner of each frame.

🎁

Christmas morning

The gift-opening sequence fits a 9-photo grid. Put the quiet “before” photo first, the happiest reaction last, and leave the centre cell for the family portrait.

💌

Christmas card

Two or four photos, a 1:1 or 4:5 ratio and a short greeting. Download the PNG and send it as an e-card, or print it as the front of a physical card.

🗓️

December recap

One photo per December moment, nine in a 3 × 3 grid, posted on New Year’s Eve. It doubles as a year-in-review post without a second edit.

How to make a Christmas collage

Five steps, no account needed.

  1. Pick your photos

    Choose 2 to 20 photos from your phone or computer. Portrait and landscape shots can be mixed in one collage.

  2. Choose a ratio

    1:1 for feed posts, 4:5 for portrait posts, 9:16 for Stories, 16:9 for cards and thumbnails. 3:4 and 4:3 are there for printing.

  3. Arrange the grid

    Leave it on Auto and the editor fits the photos to the ratio. Switch to Custom to set rows and columns, from 1 × 1 up to 10 × 10.

  4. Decorate

    Add text, emoji stickers and a filter, then adjust the gutter spacing and rounded corners until the collage feels finished.

  5. Export

    Download a PNG and post it, print it, or attach it to a message. Nothing is stored on our side.

Photo order matters. Put the strongest photo first, keep the middle cells for the family portrait, and drag photos to reorder them before you decorate. A two- or four-photo collage is usually easier to read on a phone than a nine-photo grid, so start small and add photos only if the story needs them.

Christmas collage FAQ

What size should a Christmas collage be?

Use 1:1 for feed posts, 4:5 for portrait posts, 9:16 for Stories and 16:9 for card fronts or thumbnails. Choose the ratio before you add photos and the editor keeps it through export. If you need exact pixels for a platform, check its size chart first.

How many photos can I use in a Christmas collage?

Up to 20 photos in one collage. Four photos in a 2 × 2 grid suit a Christmas card; nine in a 3 × 3 grid suit a month-in-review post. The more photos you add, the smaller each cell becomes.

Is the Christmas collage maker free?

Yes. The editor is free, exports PNG without a watermark, and does not limit how many collages you make.

Do I need to sign up to make a Christmas collage?

No account, email address or social login is needed. Open the editor, add photos and download the result.

Can I make a Christmas collage on my phone?

Yes. MeCollage runs in a mobile browser, so you can pick photos from your camera roll and export a PNG without installing an app. A larger screen makes fine-tuning text and spacing easier.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. Photos are processed in your browser with Canvas and never uploaded. Closing the tab clears the current collage, so download the PNG when you are happy with it.
