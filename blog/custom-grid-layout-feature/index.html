Custom Grid Layouts: A Complete Guide to Precise Photo Collages

Published: January 28, 2026 · Updated: November 5, 2026 by MeCollage Team

Automatic layouts are great when you want a collage in seconds. But sometimes the arrangement matters as much as the photos: a 4×3 grid for a family album, a 5×2 strip for a product page, or a deliberate 2×2 for Instagram. That is exactly what MeCollage's Custom Grid Layout is for.

This guide explains how the feature works, how to plan a grid around your photos, and the design details that make a custom layout look intentional rather than accidental.

What is a custom grid in MeCollage?

A custom grid lets you choose the number of rows and columns directly instead of relying on the automatic layout. Each cell receives one photo, images are cropped to fill their cells, and the canvas keeps the aspect ratio you selected. You can combine the grid with spacing (gutter), rounded corners, filters, text and stickers.

If you need a single photo in 1:1 before assembling the collage, use the square crop tool; if you want to keep the whole frame, use the square image converter to make image square without cropping.

The editor supports 1–10 rows and 1–10 columns and up to 20 photos per collage. When your collage has fewer than 20 photos, one cell is reserved for the “+” add-photo button, so plan for that extra slot.

When should you use a custom grid?

  • Exact capacity: you have a specific number of photos and want every cell filled without empty gaps.
  • A specific format: a 3×1 banner, a 2×5 story strip, a 4×5 Instagram profile grid.
  • Rhythm and alignment: matching rows and columns make a collage feel designed rather than random.
  • Series and catalogues: consistent cells are ideal for product photos, lookbooks and step-by-step images.

How to use Custom Grid Layout

  1. Upload your photos. Click or drag up to 20 images into the editor.
  2. Open the layout controls. In the sidebar, find the grid/layout section.
  3. Switch from Auto to Custom. The row and column inputs appear.
  4. Enter your grid. For example, 2×2 for four photos, 3×2 for six, 4×3 for twelve, 5×4 for twenty.
  5. Click Apply. The canvas updates immediately; adjust the aspect ratio, gutter and corner radius to taste.
  6. Export. When everything looks right, export the collage as a PNG.

Tip: if the grid cannot hold your photos, MeCollage warns you before applying it. Reduce the number of photos, increase rows or columns, or remove the reserved add-photo cell by filling all 20 slots.

Five grid recipes that work well

2×2 — the classic square

Four strong photos, one clear focal point. A 2×2 grid works for Instagram posts, mood boards and before/after pairs. Use a tight gutter for a single image feel, or a wider gutter for a scrapbook look.

3×2 — six memories

Six cells feel balanced without becoming busy. This is a reliable layout for travel recaps, family updates and monthly photo journals.

4×3 — the album page

Twelve photos at 4:3 or 3:2 is close to a printed album spread. Keep the gutter consistent and avoid mixing too many orientations.

5×4 — the full twenty

A 5×4 grid holds the maximum twenty photos. It is ideal for a year-in-review or a dense visual index; use a slightly larger corner radius to soften the density.

1×5 or 5×1 — the strip

Strips are great for banners, website headers and story highlights. Crop each photo to its cell and keep the subject near the centre.

Design details that make a custom grid look better

  • Pick a focal photo. Give the most important image the strongest position; with an even grid, the centre or top-left usually reads first.
  • Keep the gutter consistent. Spacing is part of the design. A consistent gutter makes the grid feel deliberate.
  • Use corner radius sparingly. Rounded corners soften a dense grid; too much radius can make small photos look like buttons.
  • Match the aspect ratio to the destination. 1:1 for Instagram posts, 9:16 for stories, 16:9 for presentations, 4:5 for portrait feeds.
  • Edit before you arrange. Choose photos with compatible brightness and colour temperature; the filters can help unify the set.

Troubleshooting

“Current grid can only hold X photos”

Your rows × columns must be at least the number of photos, plus one slot for the add-photo button when you have fewer than 20 images. Increase the grid or remove a photo.

Photos look stretched or awkwardly cropped

Grid cells use a centre crop. If a face or product is off-centre, remove and re-add the photo, or choose a different aspect ratio. For precise crops, use the dedicated Square Crop tool.

Too much empty space

An even grid with fewer photos than cells leaves gaps. Either fill the cells or choose a grid that matches your photo count exactly.

Frequently asked questions

Can I mix portrait and landscape photos in one grid?

Yes. Every cell uses the same shape, so mixed orientations are cropped to fit. For the cleanest result, group similar orientations in a row or column.

Does the custom grid work with text and stickers?

Yes. Add text layers and stickers after applying the grid; they sit above the photos and are included in the export.

Can I change the grid after applying it?

Yes. Switch back to Auto or edit the rows and columns and click Apply again. Your photos stay in place.
